Global Temperature Change Across Malaysia

How a warming planet is reshaping life at home.

Part 1 — The Global Picture

The World's Big Emitters

Climate change is global, but its effects are local. A few nations emit most of the world's carbon dioxide. Malaysia, highlighted below, adds only a small share. Yet the atmosphere ignores borders. This global warming sets the stage for our story.

Part 2 — A Nation Warms

When Malaysia Tipped Into Warming

Each bar is one year since 1940. Blue years sat below the modern average. Red years ran hotter than it. Blue dominates the early decades. After about 2000, red takes over. Our country crossed into steady warming within a single lifetime.

Keeping Pace With the Planet

How does Malaysia compare with the whole world? Each line links two decade averages. One dot is global, the other is Malaysia. Both climb steadily toward the present. Malaysia started cooler but is catching up fast. We are warming alongside everyone else.

A Century of Monthly Heat

This grid holds over 1,400 months of data. Cool blues fill the early 1900s. Deep oranges crowd the recent decades. Even our cooler monsoon months are heating up. The wall of warmth is impossible to miss.

No Month Is Spared

Here the twelve months wrap into a circle. The pale ring shows 1901 to 1930. The red ring shows 1996 to 2025. The red ring sits outside the pale one everywhere. Every single month is now warmer than before.

Part 3 — Mapping the Heat

Which States Run Hottest

Heat is not spread evenly across the country. Northern Penang, Perlis and Kedah run warmest. Highland Pahang and East Malaysia stay a little cooler. This map shows each state's average over thirty years. Darker shades mark the hotter regions.

How Warm Our Cities Run

Now we zoom from states down to cities. Each circle sits on a real weather station. Bigger, redder circles mark the hottest surroundings. George Town and Kangar lead the pack. City dwellers feel this warmth every single day.

Which Cities Run Hot or Cool

Not every city feels the heat equally. Each dot is a real weather station. Red dots run hotter than the national average. Blue dots sit a little cooler. George Town and Kangar are the clear hotspots.

Part 4 — Why It Is Happening

Our Hottest Years Are Recent

Which years broke the records? Nine of the ten warmest fell after 2015. The year 2024 stands far above the rest. The pattern is clear and recent. The heat is not a distant threat anymore.

Five Decades of Emissions

What fuels this warming at home? This stream shows our greenhouse gases over time. The band swells enormously from 1970 onward. Carbon dioxide is by far the largest driver. Our emissions have grown fourfold in fifty years.

Tracing the Flow of Carbon

Where do these gases actually come from? This diagram traces activities into broad sectors. Hover a band to follow its path. The energy sector absorbs the thickest flows. Electricity and transport are the leading sources.

Where Our Emissions Come From

Each square below equals one percent of emissions. Energy alone fills nearly four fifths of the grid. Industry, waste and farming share the rest. To cool our future, we must target energy first. The chart makes that priority obvious.
